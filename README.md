Diagram Overview
This diagram visually presents the key modules and the process flow involved in a Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Core System—from initial beneficiary registration to securing data and integrating external services.

Step-by-Step Breakdown
1. Beneficiary Register

What it is: The starting point where beneficiaries (e.g., citizens or applicants for social welfare) register themselves in the system.

Purpose: Collect initial data needed for future processing.

2. Verification

What it is: After registration, the system verifies the provided details—e.g., checks identity documents, eligibility, etc.

Purpose: Ensure only valid beneficiaries are processed, minimizing errors or fraud.

3. Document Upload

What it is: Beneficiaries upload all required documents (ID, proofs, etc.) for records and further checks.

Purpose: Capture and store necessary information for scrutiny and approval.

4. Sanction Workflow

What it is: This module manages the processing, review, and approval of sanctions—i.e., determines if the beneficiary qualifies for benefit transfer.

Purpose: Ensure compliance and transparency in benefit approval.

5. Payments Transfer

What it is: Approved payments are transferred directly to the beneficiary’s account.

Purpose: Realize the DBT goal—direct, transparent, and fast benefit delivery.

6. Real-Time Dashboard

What it is: Administrative users can monitor all processes, payouts, and issues in real time.

Purpose: Enhance oversight, identify bottlenecks, and promote good governance.

7. Grievance Module

What it is: Handles complaints, issues, or grievances of the beneficiaries.

Purpose: Offer redressal mechanism and support to the public.

8. Secure Storage

What it is: All beneficiary data, documents, and processing states are stored securely with access control and possibly encryption.

Purpose: Ensure data privacy and compliance with regulations.

9. API Links

What it is: The DBT Core System connects to external systems—such as Aadhaar, banks, or other government services—using APIs (Application Programming Interfaces).

Purpose: Enable seamless data sharing, validation, and updates.

Visual Flow
The modules are depicted linearly along a timeline, showing a logical sequence. Icons, colors, and brief captions keep the explanation intuitive. Each step flows into the next, ensuring a structured, auditable, and efficient benefit transfer process from registration to grievance resolution and data security.

In summary:
The diagram outlines each fundamental part of the DBT ecosystem and shows how they interact for end-to-end beneficiary empowerment and robust delivery of entitlements.
